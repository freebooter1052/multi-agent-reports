# Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies
**Authors:** Peter Kirgis, Sayash Kapoor, Andrew Schwartz, Stephan Rabanser, David Africa, Konstantinos Voudouris, Viet Nguyen, Toby Pilditch, Magda Dubois, Harry Coppock, Cozmin Ududec, Nitya Nadgir, Matilda Orona, Tilman Bayer, Derrick Chan-Sew, Yue Ling, Abhishek Shetty, Helen Toner, Gillian Hadfield, Seth Lazar, Steve Newman, Shoshannah Tekofsky, Rishi Bommasani, Arvind Narayanan
**Date Published:** 2026-07-29
**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191v1

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement
- The main challenge is figuring out if AI agents (smart computer programs) can actually do open-ended scientific research on their own. Right now, most tests either only give them very specific, narrow tasks or rely on slow and sometimes unreliable human reviewers to judge their work.
- This is important because many experts believe that future breakthroughs in AI depend on these agents being able to automatically conduct complex research without humans holding their hands.

## 🏗️ Architectural Overview & Technical Design
- The researchers created a new way to test AI called "shadow evaluations." Here, an AI agent is asked to solve the exact same main question as an upcoming, high-quality human research paper.
- The original human authors then look at the AI's final work and grade it, comparing it directly to what humans can do.
- The system just takes existing powerful AI models, gives them access to computers and the internet (thousands of dollars worth of computing power), and lets them work for six days straight to see what they produce.

## 🛠️ Solution Approaches & Methodology
- The authors tested these AI agents on two real, unpublished research papers. The AIs had six days to try and answer the main research questions all by themselves.
- While the AIs successfully handled all the computer programming (engineering) parts, they failed to make any real progress on answering the actual scientific questions.
- Because of this, the human authors rejected both of the AI-generated papers. The study pinpointed key mistakes the AIs kept making, like not knowing when their work was good enough, getting stuck without being able to think of new ideas, and forgetting their original instructions.

## 🌍 Societal Impact & Sector Benefits
- This study gives the tech industry a reality check: while AI can write code well, it still struggles with deep, creative scientific thinking.
- In the real world, this means developers know exactly what weaknesses they need to fix next before we can rely on AI to invent new technologies or cure diseases entirely on their own.
