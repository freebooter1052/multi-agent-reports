# RestoreBench: Can AI Agents Restore Power Flow Convergence?
**Authors:** Riccardo Mansutti, Andrea Pomarico, Robert Jakob, Qian Zhang, Alberto Berizzi, Kevin O'Sullivan
**Date Published:** 2026-08-31
**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00384v1

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement
- Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly automate multi-step engineering workflows through tool use, interpretation of intermediate results, and iterative planning.
- Diagnosing and resolving non-convergent power flow cases is a promising yet largely unexplored application, as it requires engineering judgment, experimentation, and decision-making within constrained action spaces.

## 🏗️ Architectural Overview & Technical Design
- Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly automate multi-step engineering workflows through tool use, interpretation of intermediate results, and iterative planning.
- We introduce a benchmark that evaluates these capabilities across multiple LLMs and three architectures: \emph{chatbot}, \emph{single agent}, and \emph{multi-agent} systems.

## 🛠️ Solution Approaches & Methodology
- We introduce a benchmark that evaluates these capabilities across multiple LLMs and three architectures: \emph{chatbot}, \emph{single agent}, and \emph{multi-agent} systems.
- The benchmark defines the simulation environment, observation and action spaces, and evaluation metrics, providing a reproducible foundation for developing agentic AI systems for power system planning and operation.
